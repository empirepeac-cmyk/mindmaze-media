# Long video #1: "11 Weird Things Your Brain Does That Everyone Experiences"

Idea (Sepehr, 2026-11-03): relatable "that's me!" moments. Channel data agrees: the most-viewed series episodes were relatable ones (faces everywhere ~1K, everyone watching you 660).
Target length: about 9 minutes. Each item is ~45 to 50 s: a real scene (stock footage) the viewer recognises, then a short "why your brain does it", then a one-line takeaway.
Order: strongest "that's me" first to hold viewers, best one at the end.
Narration (VO) is in English. On-screen text is in [brackets].

## 1. You walk into a room and forget why
Scene: person stops in a doorway, looks around, confused.
VO: "You walk into the kitchen... and nothing. Why are you here?"
Why: "It's called the doorway effect. Your brain files memories by location; walking through a door closes one 'scene' and opens a new one, and the plan stays behind in the old room."
Takeaway: "Walk back to where you had the thought. It usually comes back."

## 2. The falling feeling just as you fall asleep
Scene: someone in bed, a sudden jolt.
VO: "You're drifting off, and suddenly you're falling, and your whole body jumps."
Why: "A hypnic jerk. As your muscles relax, your brain misreads it as falling and slams on the brakes. Most people have had one at least once."

## 3. Your phone vibrated. It didn't.
Scene: hand reaching for the pocket, the screen is empty.
VO: "You feel your phone buzz. You check. Nothing."
Why: "Phantom vibration. Your brain is so tuned to that signal that it starts hearing it in random noise."

## 4. A song stuck in your head for days
Scene: person humming while doing something else.
Why: "An earworm. More than 9 in 10 people get one every week. Your brain keeps looping a short piece of the song, often the part it can't finish."
Takeaway: "In one study, chewing gum cut earworms by about a third. Your mouth is busy, so your inner voice can't sing along."

## 5. A word stops making sense
On screen: the word "spoon" repeated until it looks wrong.
VO: "Say 'spoon' out loud twenty times... spoon, spoon, spoon... Weird, right?"
Why: "Semantic satiation. The neurons that link the sound to the meaning get tired."

## 6. You win arguments in the shower
Scene: person in the shower (tasteful), talking to themself.
VO: "Hours after an argument, you find the perfect comeback. In the shower."
Why: "The relaxed brain makes looser connections. The French even have a word for it: l'esprit de l'escalier, the wit of the staircase."

## 7. You feel someone staring at you
Scene: person on a train turns around; someone is looking.
Why: "Your brain has a dedicated system for detecting eyes, and it's very sensitive. Also, when you turn around, the other person looks at you: so you remember the hits."

## 8. You see faces in everything
Scenes: faces in sockets, clouds, house fronts.
Why: "Pareidolia. Missing a real face was more dangerous than seeing a fake one, so your brain errs on the side of faces."

## 9. You've lived this moment before
VO: "Déjà vu: this exact moment, already happened."
Why: "One leading explanation: the place or moment looks a lot like something you've seen before, but you can't recall what. So you get the feeling of familiarity without the memory behind it."

## 10. Learn a new word, then see it everywhere
Why: "The Baader-Meinhof phenomenon. It was always there; your brain just started paying attention."

## 11. You remember the one awkward thing you said in 2014
Scene: someone in bed at night, staring at the ceiling, cringing.
VO: "It's 2 a.m. and your brain decides to replay something embarrassing you said years ago."
Why: "Your brain stores emotional moments more strongly, and at night, with nothing else to process, it rewinds them. Everyone else forgot it. Only you remember."
